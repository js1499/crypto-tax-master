Corrections & Clarifications The DeFi Desk regrets the industry's errors

In crypto tax software, the following errors are routine. Glide's editors catch them before they reach your return.

No. 01

Spam airdrops

Worthless junk tokens were booked as taxable income at fantasy prices. They are not income. Glide's spam guard filters them out. — Corrected.

No. 02

Staking & rewards

Staking rewards went entirely unreported until an auditor found them. Glide classifies them as income at the price of their block. — Corrected.

No. 03

Bridged assets

A bridged asset arrived with no cost basis, so a zero was guessed and the whole sale became "profit." Glide carries basis across wallets and chains, and surfaces what's missing rather than inventing it. — Corrected.

No. 04

Unlisted tokens

A token with no exchange listing was priced at $0, quietly. The other side of that swap had a market price; Glide uses that liquid leg. — Corrected.

No. 05

Unmapped types

Wraps, LP moves, and odd contract calls were dumped into "other" and left there. Glide maps them to real categories instead. — Corrected.

No. 06

Per-wallet basis

One pooled cost basis was spread across every wallet, which stopped matching IRS rules in 2025. Glide tracks basis wallet by wallet. — Corrected.

Companies & Markets The accuracy desk

The price was wrong. Here is the right one.

Most tools price a trade from a snapshot candle — one for the entire hour, sometimes one for the day — and pick a point in that range. Your actual trade happened at a specific second, on a specific block.

Glide prices both legs of every swap at the exact on-chain block timestamp — at fair market value, as the IRS requires — reading 180 one-second candles across 3 minutes rather than one hourly bar. On a single 100 SOL trade the difference is $62* — and it compounds with every trade you make.
